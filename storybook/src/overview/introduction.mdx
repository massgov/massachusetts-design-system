# Massachusetts Design System resources

Find reusable components, design guidance, and implementation resources for creating consistent and accessible digital experiences across the Commonwealth.

**New to the Design System?**

[Learn more about the Massachusetts Design System](https://www.mass.gov/massachusetts-design-system)

> #### The code resources are in beta and ready to use.
>
> Our design tokens and Figma component libraries are stable. We are progressively adding coded versions of those components to Storybook.

---

## Use the Design System progressively

The <a href="https://www.mass.gov/policy-advisory/website-domain-policy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Website Domain Policy</a> sets requirements for constituent-facing digital experiences, including accessibility and design. The Massachusetts Design System provides shared styles, components, patterns, and guidance to help teams meet those requirements.

You do not need to adopt the entire Design System at once. Start with the resources that fit your project, platform, timeline, and stage of development.

Use shared components and guidance when you build new features or make improvements. Design tokens can also help existing experiences move toward the system's visual language without requiring a full redesign.

---

## Choose an approach for your project

### If you are starting a new project

Use the Design System as the starting point when your team controls the front-end experience. Use its components and guidance when they meet your project's needs.

If platform, technology, or other constraints prevent you from using them, follow the system's guidance where possible and look for opportunities to improve alignment over time.

### If your project is already in progress

You do not need to stop work or redesign an established experience to adopt the Design System.

Continue meeting Commonwealth accessibility and constituent-facing design requirements. As you add features, replace components, or make other improvements, look for opportunities to adopt Design System resources as part of that work.

### If your project has already launched

You do not need to retrofit a live experience solely to match the latest Design System resources.

When you make planned updates, use them as opportunities to adopt Design System guidance and components that support your service and meet current requirements.

### If you are using a low-code or no-code platform

Use Design System guidance and visual direction where your platform allows it.

You do not need to recreate or override built-in platform behavior when doing so would introduce accessibility problems, significant maintenance work, or make future platform updates harder to manage.

Focus on the parts of the experience you can control, such as content, hierarchy, visual choices, and interaction patterns.

## Design with the Figma libraries

Use the Massachusetts Design System Figma libraries alongside Storybook to design and build digital experiences. The Figma libraries include component specifications that teams can use when a corresponding coded component is not yet available.

Figma access is available to Commonwealth state agencies and vendor partners working with them.

Learn more about using the Massachusetts Design System Figma libraries for <a href="https://www.mass.gov/info-details/designer-resources-for-the-massachusetts-design-system" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Designers</a>, or <a href="https://www.mass.gov/info-details/developer-resources-for-the-massachusetts-design-system" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Developers</a>.

---

## If a coded component is not available

You do not need to wait for a coded component to move your project forward. If a component is available in Figma, use its design specifications to build it for your application. You can also extend existing components to meet your project's needs.

<a href="https://www.mass.gov/forms/help-us-improve-our-storybook-resources" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Share feedback</a> about components, guidance, examples, or other resources you cannot find. We review feedback to understand which resources teams need and inform the Design System roadmap.
